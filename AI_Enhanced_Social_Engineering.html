Evaluating Human and Artificial Intelligence Detection of Traditional and AI-Enhanced Social Engineering Attacks

Recommended implementation plan for a master's-level cybersecurity research project, including research design, environment setup, data generation, participant evaluation, AI detection, and statistical analysis.

1. Research Goal

This project investigates how effectively human participants and artificial intelligence systems can identify social-engineering messages, particularly when phishing messages have been generated or improved using large language models.

The core research question is whether traditional phishing indicators—such as poor grammar, awkward wording, or unusual message structure—remain reliable when attackers can use modern LLMs to produce more professional and convincing messages.

Recommended study design: Compare human and AI detection performance across three message categories: legitimate messages, traditional phishing messages, and AI-enhanced phishing messages.

2. Research Questions and Hypotheses

  • RQ1: How accurately can humans identify AI-enhanced phishing emails?
  • RQ2: How accurately can an AI-based detector identify the same phishing emails?
  • RQ3: Is there a statistically significant difference between human and AI detection accuracy?
  • RQ4: Which message characteristics influence human decisions?
  • RQ5: Which message characteristics influence AI decisions?
  • RQ6: Do traditional phishing indicators such as grammar errors become less useful when messages are improved by LLMs?

Example Hypothesis

AI-enhanced phishing messages will be more difficult for human participants to identify than traditional phishing messages because AI reduces obvious linguistic indicators such as poor grammar, spelling mistakes, and awkward sentence structure.

3. Safe Research Environment

The experiment should be conducted in an isolated research environment. All messages should be synthetic and should use fake organizations, fake identities, and nonfunctional links. No simulated phishing message should be sent to real users outside the approved study environment.

17. Analyze Confidence Versus Correctness

The student should also examine whether participants are highly confident when they make incorrect judgments. 

AI-Enhanced Phishing

Correct + high confidence       35%
Correct + low confidence        25%
Incorrect + low confidence      15%
Incorrect + high confidence     25%

The final category is especially important because users who confidently misclassify phishing messages as legitimate may represent a greater organizational security risk.

18. Complete Experimental Pipeline

Define research questions ↓ Design social-engineering scenarios ↓ Create legitimate control messages ↓ Create traditional phishing messages ↓ Use local LLM to create AI-enhanced versions ↓ Validate and label dataset ↓ IRB / human-subject approval ↓ Randomized human experiment ↓ AI detector evaluation ↓ Collect human + AI predictions ↓ Calculate performance metrics ↓ Analyze message indicators ↓ Statistical comparison ↓ Identify differences between human and AI detection ↓ Develop cybersecurity-training recommendations

19. Recommended Research Positioning

A stronger version of the project is to frame it not simply as Humans vs. AI, but as:

Humans vs. AI across traditional and AI-enhanced social-engineering messages.

This creates a clearer experimental variable: the method used to create or improve the phishing message. It also makes the project better suited for statistical comparison and provides a stronger basis for developing practical cybersecurity-awareness recommendations.
