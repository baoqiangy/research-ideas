Master's Research Plan

Cybersecurity Risk Assessment of Identity, Privileged Access, and Third-Party Integrations in a Canvas LMS Environment

A focused assessment of cybersecurity risks within the university's own Canvas environment, emphasizing authentication, privileged access, API/developer-key use, and LTI or other third-party integrations.

1. Feasibility Assessment

Feasible

This project is feasible because it evaluates cybersecurity risks within the university's actual Canvas environment and focuses on areas that can be examined through institutional configuration, access-management practices, integrations, documentation, and interviews with relevant IT personnel. The selected domains—identity and authentication, privileged access, API/developer-key security, and third-party/LTI integrations—are closely related and provide a focused basis for a practical risk assessment.

Why this scope works

  • Identity and privileged access are central to LMS security.
  • API and developer-key use can be studied through public Canvas documentation.
  • LTI and third-party integrations introduce clear governance and data-access risks.
  • the researcher can produce specific, evidence-based risk scenarios without penetration testing.

2. Recommended Research Question

Primary research question:
What are the most significant cybersecurity risks associated with identity management, privileged access, API access, and third-party integrations in an institution-managed Canvas LMS environment, and what controls can reduce those risks?

Optional supporting questions

  • Which risks are primarily controlled by the institution rather than by Instructure?
  • Which weaknesses most directly increase the likelihood of account misuse, excessive access, or third-party data exposure?
  • Which NIST CSF 2.0 safeguards best address the identified risks?

3. Define the University Canvas Environment

Because the assessment will be performed within the university's own Canvas environment, document the actual environment being evaluated rather than creating a hypothetical reference institution. Describe how Canvas is configured and managed, including SSO, MFA, administrator roles, account lifecycle processes, developer keys, OAuth/API access, LTI integrations, and other relevant institutional practices. Where complete information is not available, clearly label assumptions and identify how they were derived.

Environment documentation example:
Document the university's actual Canvas deployment: who authenticates through SSO, where MFA is required, which roles have administrative privileges, how privileged access is approved and reviewed, which developer keys and OAuth integrations are active, which LTI tools are enabled, and who owns or approves each integration.

Transparent means the report clearly distinguishes verified institutional facts, published Canvas documentation, interview evidence, and any assumptions used where direct evidence is unavailable. All likelihood, impact, and residual-risk ratings should be tied to the documented university environment.

4. Final Scope

Domain A — Identity and Authentication

  • SSO
  • MFA
  • Account lifecycle
  • Compromised credentials
  • Inactive or orphaned accounts

Domain B — Privileged Access

  • Canvas administrator roles
  • Excessive privilege
  • Privileged-account review
  • Separation of duties
  • Termination or role-change access removal

Domain C — API and Developer-Key Security

  • OAuth tokens
  • Developer keys
  • API scopes
  • Credential exposure
  • Over-permissioned integrations

Domain D — Third-Party and LTI Integrations

  • LTI 1.3 applications
  • Vendor access to LMS data
  • Integration approval
  • Third-party security review
  • Revocation and decommissioning

5. Research Objectives

  1. Identify assets directly related to identity, privileged access, API access, and third-party integrations.
  2. Develop 8–10 concrete cybersecurity risk scenarios.
  3. Identify threats, weaknesses, and relevant safeguards for each scenario.
  4. Evaluate likelihood and impact using a documented risk-scoring method.
  5. Map existing and recommended safeguards to NIST CSF 2.0.
  6. Prioritize the risks in a risk register.
  7. Develop a practical risk-treatment plan focused on institution-controlled actions.

6. Suggested 8–10 Risk Scenarios

IDRisk Scenario
R1A Canvas administrator account is compromised and used to access or change sensitive LMS information or settings.
R2An administrator has more privileges than required, increasing the impact of misuse or account compromise.
R3A former employee or transferred staff member retains privileged Canvas access because account lifecycle controls are incomplete.
R4Weak or inconsistently enforced MFA increases the likelihood of unauthorized access to institutional Canvas accounts.
R5An over-scoped developer key allows an application to access more Canvas data or functions than necessary.
R6An OAuth or API token is exposed, copied, or misused by an unauthorized party.
R7An LTI application is granted excessive permissions or unnecessary data access.
R8A third-party LTI vendor is compromised, exposing institutional or user information.
R9A new integration is enabled without sufficient security and privacy review.
R10An obsolete integration or credential remains active after the application is no longer needed.

8. Core Methodology

NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1

Use for threat identification, vulnerabilities or predisposing conditions, likelihood, impact, and risk determination.

NIST CSF 2.0

Use to organize institution-controlled safeguards and recommend target-state controls.

The project should not claim that a missing public statement proves that a control is absent. Vendor documentation, institutional assumptions, and verified evidence must remain clearly separated.

10. Step-by-Step Research Plan

1

Finalize scope and document the university Canvas environment

Define the university Canvas environment, system boundary, assumptions, evidence sources, and intended audience.

Deliverable: 1–2 page assessment charter.
2

Conduct literature and documentation review

Review Canvas security documentation, developer documentation, NIST standards, EDUCAUSE guidance, and scholarly research.

"Canvas LMS" AND security
"LTI" AND security
"OAuth" AND LMS
"developer key" AND Canvas
"higher education" AND third-party risk
"privileged access" AND LMS
Deliverable: evidence and literature matrix.
3

Create the asset inventory

Limit the inventory to identities, administrator accounts, tokens, developer keys, LTI configurations, integration credentials, and data reachable through those mechanisms.

Deliverable: focused asset inventory.
4

Create the architecture and trust-boundary diagram

Model the identity provider, users, Canvas, administrators, API clients, SIS, and LTI applications. Mark trust boundaries among the institution, Instructure, end users, and third parties.

Deliverable: one architecture/data-flow diagram.
5

Develop 8–10 concrete risk scenarios

Use a cause–event–impact format. Each scenario should identify the affected asset, threat event, weakness, and consequence.

Deliverable: risk-scenario catalog.
6

Build the control checklist

Identify relevant controls such as MFA, least privilege, privileged-access review, developer-key scoping, token revocation, integration approval, LTI governance, and third-party review.

Deliverable: control checklist with NIST CSF 2.0 mapping.
7

Score risks

Use a consistent 5×5 likelihood and impact model. Score inherent risk, document applicable controls, then estimate residual risk.

Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact

1–4   = Low
5–9   = Moderate
10–16 = High
17–25 = Critical

Every score must include a short rationale and evidence source.

Deliverable: completed risk register.
8

Validate assumptions

If interviews are approved, interview a small number of LMS administrators, IAM staff, or cybersecurity professionals. Use interviews to validate assumptions and common practices rather than request confidential details.

Deliverable: assumption-validation table or interview themes.
9

Perform sensitivity analysis

Change the likelihood of High/Critical risks by ±1 and determine whether the ranking changes substantially.

Deliverable: sensitivity table.
10

Develop the risk-treatment plan

Recommend institution-controlled actions for the highest-priority risks.

  • Require MFA for privileged access.
  • Conduct periodic privileged-access reviews.
  • Limit and document developer-key scopes.
  • Establish token rotation and revocation processes.
  • Maintain an approved integration inventory.
  • Require formal review before enabling new LTI applications.
  • Remove unused integrations and credentials.
Deliverable: prioritized treatment roadmap.

14. Final Recommendation

Proceed with this narrowed topic. It gives the researcher enough technical depth for a master's project while keeping the scope realistic. The strongest contribution is a reproducible, evidence-based risk assessment of institution-controlled access and integration risks in Canvas rather than a broad judgment about whether Canvas itself is secure.
